Phase 1 gets you solving; this lesson strips the wasted motion out of it.
Three upgrades, and none is genuinely new — each reuses a trigger you already
own. Hold the cube with **white on the bottom, yellow on top** from the first
move to the last: after this lesson you never flip it again.

## 1. Build the white cross on the bottom

Stop building the cross on top and turning the cube over. Build it straight
onto the bottom face, with yellow up the whole time.

The recipe: get a white edge into the **top layer** with its white sticker
facing up, turn U until its side color sits directly above the matching
center, then double-turn that face — the edge drops home without disturbing
the others. An edge stuck in the bottom layer facing the wrong way? Double-turn
its face to pop it up, then place it properly. If the white sticker faces
sideways instead of up, insert it the way you did in Phase 1 — intuitively —
just aimed downwards.

Why bother: the flip cost you a whole-cube rotation *and* a re-read of every
face. Killing it means one grip for the entire solve, and the solved cross
stays safely out of sight while you work above it.

## 2. Yellow cross: give the Hook its own algorithm

Lowercase means **wide**: `f` turns the front *two* layers together — the F
face plus the slice behind it. So f-sexy-f',
<AlgText moves="f R U R' U' f'" />, is just F-sexy-F' with a deeper grip.
Same fingers, same trigger, nothing new to memorize — but it solves the Hook
in one go instead of two.

- **Hook** — hold the yellow L in the **front-right**, run f-sexy-f'. Done in
  one algorithm instead of Phase 1's two.
- **Line** — unchanged: hold it horizontal, F-sexy-F'.
- **Dot** — Line algorithm, then Hook algorithm, back to back with no re-grip
  in between. Two algorithms instead of three, and no re-reading the cube
  mid-case.

<Callout type="tip">
  The Dot chain works because F-sexy-F' turns a Dot into a Hook that is
  already sitting at the correct angle — start the wide-f version immediately.
</Callout>

## 3. Orient corners without the flip

Everything up to the last step is unchanged: yellow cross, edges aligned,
corners positioned. The old Step 7 flipped the cube; skip that. Yellow stays
on top.

Turn U until an unsolved corner — one with no yellow on top — sits at the
**front-right**. Read where its yellow sticker points:

<Figure src="/diagrams/steps/orient_right.svg" alt="Front-right corner with yellow sticker facing right" caption="Yellow right — R' D' R D, twice" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/steps/orient_front.svg" alt="Front-right corner with yellow sticker facing front" caption="Yellow front — D' R' D R, twice" />

Yellow to the **right** is <AlgText moves="R' D' R D" /> twice; yellow to the
**front** is <AlgText moves="D' R' D R" /> twice — the first algorithm undone,
the same moves inverted in reverse order.

Then turn **only U** to bring the next unsolved corner to the front-right and
repeat. When all four corners show yellow, a final U turn or two lines the
side colors up — solved.

<Callout type="caution">
  The bottom two layers look wrecked between corners. This is normal. Turn
  only U between corners — never rotate the cube or touch any other face —
  and everything snaps back once the last corner is done.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  It repairs itself for the same reason Step 7 did: six identical rounds
  return the cube exactly to where it started, and corner twists always
  cancel in multiples of three. Any move other than U in between breaks that
  accounting.
</Callout>
