The beginner method fills the first two layers in two passes: white corners,
then middle edges — one algorithm each, slot by slot. **F2L** (First Two
Layers) merges the passes: each corner travels with its middle edge as a
**pair**, and both drop into place in one short motion. Same four slots,
roughly half the moves — and the core of it needs no new algorithms, only
new eyes.

## What a pair is

On a solved cube, every white corner sits directly under the middle-layer
edge that shares its two side colors: the white-red-green corner lives
below the red-green edge. Those two pieces are a **pair**; the spot they
fill is a **slot**. Cross plus four pairs is the entire first two layers.

You have been handling pairs all along. One sexy move,
<AlgText moves="R U R' U'" />, pops the front-right pair out of its slot —
corner and edge leave home **together** and land on top still joined:

Played in reverse, the same motion puts the pair back. Every F2L insert is
a version of that reversal.

## The four slots: FR, FL, BR, BL

Slots are named by position: **front-right, front-left, back-right,
back-left**. Two rules of grip discipline:

- The cross stays on the **bottom**. Never tilt the cube to look — read
  the top layer and the sides.
- Work the active slot at the **front-right**: `y` rotations bring any
  slot there. Back slots are the same slots with worse visibility, so
  clear them early or rotate them to the front.

## The three states of a pair

Find a white corner on the top layer, then find its edge. You will see one
of three states:

- **Joined, correctly** — side by side, matching colors touching, white
  facing sideways. Ready to insert as-is.
- **Joined, wrong** — side by side, but the touching colors clash or white
  faces up. Pull them apart first — one face turn lifts the corner away —
  then rejoin.
- **Split** — corner and edge in different places on top. Steer one next
  to the other so the matching colors meet: now it is a made pair.

All roads lead to the first state. F2L in one sentence: make the pair,
then insert it.

## The two basic insertions

A made pair enters its slot through one of the slot's two faces, and the
pair itself tells you which. Read the color on top of the pair: it matches
one of the slot's two centers — the pair goes in through the **other**
face.

Open, carry, close — three beats either way, and which face opens is the
only decision. That is the whole engine. Every awkward position — white on
top, corner stuck in a slot, flipped edge — is a short setup that produces
a made pair pointing one of these two ways.

## An empty slot is workspace

Joining a split pair needs room to move without wrecking what is solved.
An **empty slot** is that room: turns that open an unsolved slot cost
nothing, while turns that open a solved one must be paid back. Early in
the solve you have workspace everywhere; the last pair is the cramped
one — another reason to clear the back slots early.

<Callout type="tip">
  Count the difference once and you will not go back. The beginner method
  spends around twenty moves per slot; an intuitive pair averages seven or
  eight — the same two layers for roughly half the moves, with nothing new
  memorized.
</Callout>

Intuition covers most pairs you will meet. The awkward minority — corner
trapped in a slot, edge flipped in place — fall to the same logic plus a
setup move, and speedsolvers have worked out efficient answers for all
**41 F2L cases**. Learn the logic first; look up the exceptions later.
