A 4×4 is your 3×3 plus two new skills, plus parity you'll learn to expect.
Skill one is **centers**; skill two, pairing edges, comes next. On a 3×3
the center *was* the face's color — fixed, unarguable. On a 4×4 every
center is four loose pieces that travel like anything else. Nothing on the
cube tells you which face is white — except the corners.

## Wide moves

Big-cube algorithms turn layers in groups. `Rw` turns the outer **two**
right layers together; `3Rw` turns **three** — on a 4×4, everything but the
left layer. The number counts layers in from the named face, and plain `R`
is still just the outer layer. Everything else — `'`, `2`, other faces like
`Uw` — works exactly as on the 3×3.

## The corners carry the color scheme

A corner's three colors are molded together: white-red-green stays
white-red-green forever, and exactly one corner has that triple. Centers
can lie about where a color belongs; corners can't. Every center you build
gets checked against them.

## Build order

1. **White first.** Collect the four white center pieces into one 2×2
   block on any face. Two pieces make a 1×2 bar; build a second bar and
   join them with a wide turn.
2. **Yellow opposite.** Same technique, forced destination: yellow sits
   directly across from white, always.
3. **Read the sides from a corner.** Hold white down. Take any corner with
   a white sticker and turn the cube until it sits in the bottom
   front-right with white facing the floor. Its two side stickers *name*
   those faces: the white-red-green corner says red front, green right.
   Opposites fill in the rest — orange back, blue left.
4. **Build the four side centers** where the corner said they belong. Bars
   again: form one, park it with a face turn, form the second, join. When
   a wide turn borrows a slice from a finished center, give it back with
   the inverse before touching anything else on that axis. Build two
   *adjacent* sides first, so the final pair is adjacent too.

<Callout type="tip">
  One reading is enough: with white down, the side colors always run
  **red → green → orange → blue** clockwise around the white face. Learn
  that ring once and any single white corner orients the whole cube.
</Callout>

## Two shortcuts for the last two centres

The build order above finishes every case on its own — these two only make the
two most annoying last-two-centres positions fast. They are optional, and they
are not course algorithms.

<AlgText moves="Rw' F2 Rw2 U2 Rw'" /> swaps pieces between the **U and F**
centres, leaving R, L, D and B untouched.

<AlgText moves="Rw2 U2 D2 Rw2" /> swaps pieces between the **U and D** centres,
leaving F, B, L and R untouched.

<Callout type="caution">
  Both wreck the corners and edges — measured, four to eight corners and twelve
  to sixteen wings move. That is fine here and only here: centres come first,
  so nothing else is placed yet. Never reach for them later in a solve. They
  also do **not** carry across to a 5×5: there they reach only half of each
  centre, so they finish nothing.
</Callout>

## The last two centers

Rotate the whole cube so the two unfinished centers sit **front** and
**top** — the four finished ones are now safe on the other faces. The
insert is a three-beat rhythm, and this is it:

The middle beat is the one you choose: turn the top to drop the piece you
need into that column, or to clear one you don't. Repeat until both faces
are solid.

<Callout type="caution">
  A misplaced center is cheap now and brutal later: two colors built on
  swapped faces look fine until the 3×3 stage refuses to finish, and by
  then the fix is tearing the swapped centers back down — and re-pairing
  every edge the repair breaks. Before you pair a single edge, hold white
  down, find a white corner, and confirm every side center matches the
  corner stickers beside it.
</Callout>
