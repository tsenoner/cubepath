After step 6 every piece sits in its correct slot — the only flaw left is
that some yellow corners are twisted in place. This step untwists them with
nothing but the righty trigger.

## Flip the cube

Turn the whole cube over: `x2`. Yellow is now on the **bottom**, white back
on top. Keep exactly this grip for the entire step.

## The routine

<Figure
  size="side"
  src="/diagrams/steps/orient_corner.svg"
  alt="Cube held yellow-down with a twisted corner at the front-right"
  caption="Yellow down, the twisted corner at front-right."
/>

1. Turn `D` until a twisted corner — one whose yellow sticker does **not**
   point straight down — sits at the **front-right**.
2. Do the righty: <AlgText moves="R U R' U'" />. Work in pairs: after two
   triggers, look under the corner. Yellow facing down? Done. Not yet? Two
   more. Every corner takes exactly two or four.
3. Turn **only `D`** to bring the next twisted corner to the front-right.
   Repeat.
4. When all four corners show yellow underneath, turn `D` to line the bottom
   layer up with the rest. Solved.

<Callout type="caution">
  Mid-step the cube looks scrambled — white corners drift off, the sides
  break apart. **This is normal.** Never rotate the whole cube and never turn
  any face except `D` until every corner is done. One stray turn and the mess
  stops repairing itself.
</Callout>

## Why the mess repairs itself

Six righty triggers in a row return the cube exactly to where it started.
Each corner costs two or four, and across a full set of twisted corners the
total is always a multiple of six — so everything the trigger disturbs cycles
home by the time the last corner is done. The "only `D` between
corners" rule exists to protect that cycle.

## You can solve the cube

Congratulations — that's the whole method: cross, corners, edges, yellow
cross, align edges, position corners, orient corners. Before moving on, do
**five full solves without looking anything up** — the steps must come from
your hands, not the page. Then Phase 1.5 makes the same solve faster with
zero new algorithms.
