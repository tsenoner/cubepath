Today the last-layer order changes, permanently: **orient edges → orient
corners → permute corners → permute edges**. All orientation first, then all
permutation. This is the CFOP order, and nothing you learn later will
rearrange it again.

Why it wins: in the beginner order the yellow face came together last, so
every step meant re-scanning a half-scrambled top. Orientation-first means
that once the corners are done, the top is solid yellow — every remaining
decision reads off side stickers alone. The finished method is still only
about eighteen algorithms all told; the switch itself costs two.

Grip stays the same throughout: **white on the bottom, yellow on top**.
Nothing changes before the last layer — solve the cross, the first two
layers, and the yellow cross exactly as before. The switch starts here.

## 1. Orient corners: chain Sune

Yellow cross done. Look at the four top corners: some show yellow on top,
the rest have their yellow sticker twisted onto a side. You already own the
tool that fixes this.

</div>

If you see exactly the Sune case — one yellow corner in the front-left, the
other three twisted clockwise — one Sune finishes the top. For **any other
corner pattern**, apply Sune from any angle, then look again. Repeat until
the top is solid yellow or the Sune case appears. It always converges within
a few passes, because Sune twists three corners at once while leaving the
fourth alone.

<Callout type="tip">
  Don't hunt for patterns yet. Phase 3 gives the remaining six corner cases
  their own algorithms; chaining `R U R' U R U2 R'` trades a few extra
  repetitions for zero new memorization.
</Callout>

## 2. Permute corners: T-Perm

The top is all yellow, but the corners may sit in each other's slots. Read
the **side** stickers: on each side face, check the two corner stickers. Two
matching corner stickers on one face are **headlights** — a car seen head-on.
The edge between them doesn't have to match.

Hold the headlights on the **left**, then run T-Perm — it swaps the two
right-hand corners and leaves the yellow face untouched.

<div class="case-list">
  <CaseRow id="pll.t" />
</div>

- **Headlights on one face** — hold them left, T-Perm once.
- **No headlights anywhere** — that's a diagonal swap. T-Perm from any
  angle creates headlights; hold them left, T-Perm again.
- **Headlights on every face** — corners are already permuted. Skip ahead.

<Callout type="caution">
  Niklas, your Phase 1 corner tool, would destroy the yellow face you just
  finished — it belongs before orientation, never after. T-Perm swaps corners
  while preserving every top sticker; that is exactly why it takes over here.
</Callout>

Y-Perm, the dedicated diagonal solver, arrives in Phase 3.

## 3. Permute edges: Ub

Turn U until every corner matches its side center. All that remains is three
or four top edges out of place. Find the **solved edge** — the one whose
side sticker matches the face it sits on — and hold it at the **back**.

<div class="case-list">
  <CaseRow id="pll.ub" />
</div>

- **No solved edge?** Apply Ub once from any angle — one appears. Find it,
  hold it at the back, apply again.
- **Front edge wants to go right, not left?** That's Ua, whose M-slice
  algorithm arrives in Phase 3. Until then, Ub from the same grip still
  works — it converts the case into its mirror; one more Ub finishes.

Repeat the routine — find the solved edge, back, Ub — until the cube is
solved. You will never need more than three passes.

That's the switch complete. Same solve, two new algorithms — Phase 3 only
fills in the remaining cases so each look becomes a single algorithm.
