WCA competitions give you **15 seconds of inspection** before every solve: you
may pick the cube up and rotate it, but not turn a face. Fast solvers don't
spend that time deciding whether to start — they spend it planning. Leave
inspection with the whole cross in your head, and the first seconds of the
solve run on memory instead of eyesight.

<Callout type="info">
  Starting after 15 seconds costs a +2 penalty; after 17 it's a DNF. Ignore
  the clock while learning — plan for as long as you need, and let the time
  shrink on its own.
</Callout>

## Cross on the bottom, always

Build the cross on the bottom from the very first move. You barely see it —
that's the point. Your eyes stay on the top two layers, where the F2L pieces
live, and the moment the cross is done you are already looking at your first
pair. Building on the left or front and rotating afterwards throws away the
inspection you just did.

## Count your moves

Every scramble's cross can be solved in **eight moves or fewer** — most in
five or six. That fact turns planning into a budget: if your solution runs
past eight, the edge *order* is wrong, not the scramble. Often solving a
different edge first lets one move place two edges at once. During
inspection, count the moves of your plan; hunt for the shorter one before
you commit.

## Trace two edges first

Planning four edges at once is too much at the start. Begin smaller:

1. Pick the two easiest white edges and plan them to the bottom.
2. Before turning anything, predict where your own moves will *send* the
   other two edges.
3. Execute, then check: did they land where you predicted?

That prediction — tracing pieces through moves you haven't made yet — is the
entire skill. Two edges becomes three, then four, with practice alone.

## One grip, no rotations

Plan a cross you can finish from a single grip. Rotating the cube mid-cross
destroys your mental picture and your look-ahead: every piece you traced is
suddenly somewhere else. Instead of turning the cube to reach a slot, turn
**D** to bring the slot to you, and reach the back edges with the back face
rather than spinning to face them. If your plan needs a rotation, replan the
edge order until it doesn't.

## Plan the first pair

Once the full cross fits comfortably in inspection, spend the leftover
seconds on F2L: find a corner and edge that end up together, trace where
your cross moves send them, and know which slot you'll fill first. Even a
partial read — "the white-red-green corner ends up top-right" — removes the
pause that follows the cross in most solves. "Cross + 1" planned in
inspection is the standard every fast solver holds.

## Drills

- **Blind cross.** Inspect with no timer, plan every move, close your eyes,
  execute. Open them only when you believe the cross is done. A broken cross
  means the plan was incomplete — the fix is more careful inspection, not
  slower turning.
- **Cross + 1.** Plan the cross *and* locate your first pair during
  inspection. Execute the cross without pausing, then the pair without
  searching. Count any hesitation as a miss.
- **Move budget.** For ten scrambles, write down your planned cross length.
  Average under six, never over eight.

<Callout type="tip">
  Do the blind-cross drill daily. Eyes-closed execution is the only honest
  test of a plan — with eyes open, you patch mistakes without noticing you
  made them.
</Callout>
