A 3×3 cube looks like 54 stickers that move anywhere. It isn't. It's 26 solid
pieces, and each kind can only ever trade places with its own kind.

## The three kinds of pieces

- **6 centers** — one per face, a single color. Centers **never move**
  relative to each other: the white center is always opposite yellow, red
  always opposite orange, blue always opposite green. The center *is* the
  face's color.
- **12 edges** — two stickers each. An edge lives between two centers and can
  only swap with other edges.
- **8 corners** — three stickers each. Corners only swap with corners.

<Callout type="info">
  This is the unbreakable rule: you never move a sticker, you move a piece.
  A white-red edge stays a white-red edge forever — solving means driving it
  between the white and red centers.
</Callout>

## How to hold it

Hold the cube with **white on the bottom and yellow on top** — every lesson
on this site assumes that grip. With red facing you, green is on your right
and blue on your left.

## What "solved" actually means

Each piece sits between the centers that match its stickers. That reframing
does real work: when a lesson says "put the white-red edge home," you now
know exactly where home is — between the white center and the red center —
without memorizing anything.

## If your cube seems impossible

A corner rotated in place, or an edge flipped in place, by popping and
reassembling (or by a previous owner) makes the cube **unsolvable** — no
sequence of turns fixes it. If a guide "stops working" at the last layer with
one twisted corner, pop that corner out gently and turn it back, or twist it
in place. That's repair, not cheating.
