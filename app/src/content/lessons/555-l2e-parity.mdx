Freeslicing works because a broken edge group elsewhere absorbs the damage
from your slice moves. With ten groups done, nothing is left to absorb it —
the last two edges need sequences that put the slices back themselves.
That's all L2E is: insert, then restore, in one motion.

Hold both unfinished groups where you can see them — the recognition text on
each case tells you the exact angle.

## Edge parity

Half of your solves end with this: the last edge sits in its slot looking
flipped, everything else paired. On a 3×3 that state is impossible —
flipping a single edge breaks the permanent laws of the puzzle. Here it's
expected, because wings have no orientation of their own: what looks like a
flipped piece is really a legal rearrangement of wings around the midge.
One dedicated algorithm fixes it.

## The remaining cases

The other nine cases (l2e-4 through l2e-13, minus the parity case above)
are combinations of what you just learned — flips on both groups, parity
plus a flip, both wings wrong at once. Nothing conceptually new. Learn them
from the [L2E reference](/reference/), which lists all thirteen with
recognition and 3D playback.

## Why there is no PLL parity

The 4×4 hides an ambiguity: its centers are built from four identical
pieces, so "solved" centers can be internally rearranged, and half of those
hidden arrangements force an odd edge permutation that surfaces at the end
as PLL parity. A 5×5 has a true fixed center piece on every face — the color
scheme is anchored absolutely, and midges obey the same permutation
arithmetic as 3×3 edges. Once your edges are paired against those fixed
anchors, midge and corner permutations always share the same parity — the
3×3 law holds. Every last layer you meet is a genuine 3×3 last layer,
solvable by the algorithms you already own. The flipped-looking edge above
is the only surprise a 5×5 can throw, and it lives here in L2E — not at the
end of your solve.

## You're done learning

A 3×3 solver who has worked through these lessons holds everything a 5×5
can ask: build centers, pair edges, fix the flip when it appears, and let
your 3×3 finish the solve.
