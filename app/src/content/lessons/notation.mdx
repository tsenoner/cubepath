Every algorithm is a string of symbols, and every symbol is one turn. Hold the
cube **white on bottom, yellow on top** — the grip for the whole course — and
note that letters name *positions*, not colours: `F` is whichever face points
at you right now.

## The six faces

A bare letter means one quarter turn (90°) of that face, **clockwise as if you
were looking at it head-on**.

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_r.svg" alt="R — the right face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="R — Right" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_l.svg" alt="L — the left face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="L — Left" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_u.svg" alt="U — the top face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="U — Up" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_d.svg" alt="D — the bottom face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="D — Down" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_f.svg" alt="F — the front face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="F — Front" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_b.svg" alt="B — the back face turns one quarter clockwise" caption="B — Back" />

<Callout type="caution">
  "Head-on" is the trap. `B` is clockwise for someone standing behind the
  cube — from where you sit it looks counter-clockwise. Opposite faces always
  spin opposite ways from your seat: `L` mirrors `R`, `D` mirrors `U`.
</Callout>

## Prime, double, wide

Three marks modify any letter:

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_r_prime.svg" alt="R prime — the right face turns one quarter counter-clockwise" caption="R' — counter-clockwise" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_r2.svg" alt="R2 — the right face turns a half turn" caption="R2 — half turn" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_rw.svg" alt="Lowercase r — the right two layers turn together" caption="r — wide, two layers" />

- **' (prime)** — counter-clockwise instead. `R'` exactly undoes `R`.
- **2** — a half turn (180°). Direction doesn't matter; both ways land the same.
- **lowercase** — wide: turn *two* layers, the face plus the slice behind it.

## Slices

The three middle layers have their own letters:

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_m.svg" alt="M — the middle slice between L and R, turning in the direction of L" caption="M — Middle (as L)" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_s.svg" alt="S — the standing slice between F and B, turning in the direction of F" caption="S — Standing (as F)" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_e.svg" alt="E — the equator slice between U and D, turning in the direction of D" caption="E — Equator (as D)" />

- `M` — between `L` and `R`, turns the same direction as `L`.
- `S` — between `F` and `B`, follows `F`.
- `E` — between `U` and `D`, follows `D`.

Slices move centers, so mid-algorithm the whole cube looks recoloured — that's
normal. Only `M` appears in this course, in the Phase 3 edge algorithms.

## Rotations

`x`, `y`, `z` turn the **whole cube** in your hands. No piece moves relative to
any other — a rotation is just "regrip", which is why algorithms sometimes
start with one.

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_x.svg" alt="x — rotate the whole cube in the direction of an R turn" caption="x — rotate (as R)" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_y.svg" alt="y — rotate the whole cube in the direction of a U turn" caption="y — rotate (as U)" />

<Figure src="/diagrams/notation/move_z.svg" alt="z — rotate the whole cube in the direction of an F turn" caption="z — rotate (as F)" />

- `x` — like `R`: the front face tips up. `x2` flips the cube upside down —
  Phase 1's "flip".
- `y` — like `U`: the right face swings round to the front.
- `z` — like `F`. You'll rarely need it.

## Your first trigger

Read <AlgText moves="R U R' U'" />: four symbols, four turns — `R`, `U`, `R'`,
`U'`. This is the **sexy move**, and it's the spine of nearly every algorithm
you'll learn here.

<Figure size="card" height={320} caption="The sexy move, played from a solved cube — step through it turn by turn.">
  {/* Notation demo — no stage, deliberately: the whole cube is the subject. */}
  <TwistyPlayer alg="R U R' U'" anchor="start" />
</Figure>

**Drill:** from a solved cube, repeat <AlgText moves="R U R' U'" /> six times.
The cube returns to solved — any fixed sequence eventually cycles back, and the
sexy move's cycle is exactly six. If it doesn't come back, one of your 24 turns
was wrong: slow down and re-read. Repeat until your hands run it without your
eyes.

You won't read algorithms letter by letter for long:
<AlgText moves="F R U R' U' F'" /> is six symbols but three chunks — `F`, sexy
move, `F'`. Nearly every algorithm in this course decomposes like that.
